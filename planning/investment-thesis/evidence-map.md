## Apparent tensions and resolutions

- **Idea-stage eligibility versus product gate:** apparent conflict resolved by authority. Public eligibility is broader; the human deliberately selected a stricter review workflow for this demo. Discovery remains open to ideas.
- **Technical-first sourcing versus broad mandate:** search priority is a chosen allocation of attention, not a claim that all attractive companies write software.
- **Founder emphasis versus evidence discipline:** founder promise can motivate investigation; it does not make unobserved product or identity claims true.
- **Revealed preference versus selective evidence:** portfolio winners omit rejected and unsuccessful comparisons. The historical lens remains unavailable until valid time-specific precedents are supplied.
- **Urgency versus why now:** a dated catalyst may justify attention; a production deadline does not strengthen an investment case.

## Unknowns and review decisions

Rejected-company evidence and current private decision processes are unavailable. There is no basis for calibrated scores, return predictions, numeric weights, or acceptance probabilities. No actual candidate facts are asserted in the illustrative scenarios. Provider support for product, founder identity, revenue, and YC status must be checked separately; Crustdata HTTP success alone establishes none of these capabilities.

They are public-source investigation examples, not completed qualifications or provider results. YC status is unknown for AlloDirect, MORF-Bio, and Graph; Biodock is confirmed known YC portfolio. Additional MORF product/new-funding fetches and AlloDirect's product fetch failed; these failures are evidence gaps, not negative company facts.
