## Review brief

**Core thesis:** Prioritize founders who demonstrate the ability to build useful products, learn quickly from users, and act on a distinctive insight. Investigate the strength of each case through attributable evidence. Advance product-evidenced opportunities toward partner review and human-reviewed outreach while preserving uncertainty and contrary evidence.

This draft combines an investment reference mandate with the operating rules needed to make its judgments inspectable. Sections 1–7 describe what to seek and how to judge fit. Sections 8–11 describe evidence, decisions, and candidate outputs. Sections 12–13 cover demonstration use and research limits.

The following defaults make this draft complete enough to review; they remain proposals rather than approved runtime policy:

| Review point | Proposed first-draft position |
|---|---|
| Product evidence | An attributable working product, prototype, or equivalent technical demonstration. A deck, landing page, or waitlist alone is insufficient. |
| Stage boundary | Idea, pre-seed, and seed are the search focus. Later-stage cases are flagged for review rather than automatically rejected. |
| Qualification authority | The agent recommends partner review; a human decides whether a case becomes Qualified. |
| Counted outcome | Count Outreach Ready cases toward the headline target; show intermediate cases separately. |
| Run limits | Specify a target, deadline, and resource budget before execution. Numerical values remain unset. |
| Monitoring | Every monitored case requires an observable trigger and an accountable owner. |

**Review priorities:** First assess whether the investment emphasis reflects your intent; then review the product gate and qualification boundary. Operational limits can be selected when a specific demo run is prepared. All company scenarios in this draft are illustrative.

## 1. Executive mandate

Continuously discover founders and young companies whose ability to build, learn from users, and pursue a distinctive opportunity warrants investigation. Convert promising leads into evidence-backed candidate cases that explain what exists, who built it, why it matters now, what remains uncertain, and whether a partner should review it.

The service's product is a defensible next action, rather than a ranked list alone. A successful run delivers reviewable cases or explains its shortfall. It never manufactures certainty to meet a target.

The unit of work is a **versioned scouting mandate**: intended user, stage scope, search priorities, explicit constraints, evidence gates, known-company universe, desired output, review owner, deadline, and resource limits. Targets and budgets remain unset until chosen; they are operational limits rather than company-quality criteria.

The endpoint is an **outreach-ready package for human review**. Research, identity corroboration, rationale preparation, and message drafting are in scope. External contact, investment decisions, and representations of affiliation are outside this draft's authority.

## 2. Public foundation and its limits

YC publishes meaningful guidance, including application advice, program information, investment terms, and Requests for Startups. The sources reviewed here do not provide a complete calibrated selection function. Their existence prevents us from asserting that YC has no public thesis; their limits prevent us from claiming to reconstruct one.

The current FAQ allows idea-stage and pre-revenue companies, solo founders, and businesses outside software. It stresses a founding team's ability to build its product and allows applications from around the world. It also discusses more advanced companies; it does not establish automatic funding-round exclusions. [YC FAQ, undated live page, accessed 24 October 2026](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq).

Paul Graham's application essay emphasizes specific founder accomplishments, clear explanations, distinctive insight, and awareness of obstacles. It is an authored account on YC's site, with no publication date visible in the reviewed page, rather than proof of every current partner's practice. [How to Apply to YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply).

Geoff Ralston's 25 September 2017 advice emphasizes launching, talking to users, and iterating. This supports investigating learning and user value, but startup-building advice is not itself an admissions rubric. [YC's Essential Startup Advice](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/ycs-essential-startup-advice).

Graham's October 2010 essay discusses determination, flexibility, imagination, resourcefulness, and founder relationships. Treat this as dated founder commentary, with examples subject to selection and hindsight effects. [What We Look for in Founders](https://www.paulgraham.com/founders.html).

The RFS page explicitly describes its topics as only a fraction of YC's funding interests. The Fall 2026 edition includes named requests such as Pete Koomen's cloud for small software and Aaron Epstein's multiplayer AI, alongside education and physical-world opportunities. These are dated search prompts, not mandatory sector boundaries or statements that every contributor speaks for YC's entire selection process. [Requests for Startups, Fall 2026 edition](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs).

## 4. Three lenses, kept distinct

### A. Publicly stated guidance

Store the source, date, author, type, and relevant claim. Distinguish current program information from dated advice. Preserve the distinction between eligibility, selection commentary, and thematic interest. Do not flatten these into a single label called official thesis.

### B. Historical reference patterns

Treat this lens as **historical reference patterns — uncalibrated**. A portfolio can suggest questions; it cannot establish how YC trades off characteristics without investment-time facts and rejected-company evidence.

For any future precedent, record what was knowable at the original decision date, what later became known, the claimed resemblance, and an important difference. Current success, pedigree, and publicity must not be backfilled as early evidence. No portfolio analysis has been performed for this version; this lens is unavailable rather than silently approximated.

Technical dropout status, elite employers, and uniformly low capital needs are not adopted preferences. A successful alumnus is an illustration, not a calibrated matching rule.

### C. Current candidate evidence

Evaluate the particular company at a stated time. Each material claim must point to an observation, carry a confidence label, and preserve counterevidence. Source visibility and company quality are different properties: private development or limited publicity produces an information gap.

## 5. Discovery hypotheses

The following lanes are **operator-proposed expansions** of the user's broad mandate, not YC sector quotas:

| Lane | What to look for | Useful evidence | Question that could defeat the case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developer tools and infrastructure | A recurring technical bottleneck with a working solution | Releases, documentation, reproducible demonstration, user issues | Does this solve a recurring problem or merely attract developer attention? |
| AI applications and vertical software | A specific workflow improved materially for a defined user | Workflow demonstration, integration detail, attributed user feedback | Does the benefit survive error handling, human review, and operating costs? |
| Agent collaboration and deployment | Evidence that people need to share, control, or deploy agent work | Working collaboration or deployment flow, permissions behavior, user response | Is there a distinct unmet need beyond incumbent tooling? |
| Bio, hardware, and frontier technology | A credible technical milestone and path to practical deployment | Prototype, experimental result, limitations, next milestone | What must be proved before this can become a useful product? |
| Broad opportunity lane | Strong builder/customer evidence outside priority themes | Product artifact and specific problem context | Are we dismissing a compelling case because it lacks fashionable vocabulary? |

Discovery can use company sites, public repositories, launches, technical publications, founder writing, and verified provider observations when available. GitHub and HN are useful leads but introduce software and publicity bias. X biographies and directory snippets are leads requiring corroboration. No provider capability is assumed to verify a field until checked by its integration owner.

## 6. Assessment matrix

Use independent qualitative judgments. There are no weights, hidden total score, probability of acceptance, or mandatory numerical performance thresholds.

| Dimension | Assessment question | Positive evidence | Contrary evidence or next investigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder execution and learning | Can this team turn insight into useful progress and adapt? | Attributed product changes tied to feedback; obstacles overcome | Activity without product change; clarify contribution and learning history |
| Customer problem and demand | Whose problem is acute, and what do they do today? | Concrete workflow, attributable user experience, repeat use if available | Vague audience; ask for the current workaround and evidence of pain |
| Non-obvious insight | What specific belief changes the product or go-to-market approach? | Testable explanation linked to an artifact or observation | Generic superiority claim; investigate the mechanism and alternatives |
| Product and technical leverage | What works, under which conditions, and why is it useful? | Working demonstration, prototype result, credible comparison | Benchmark without context; clarify baseline, limitations, and ownership |
| Durable advantage | What might become hard to replicate as the company learns? | Workflow integration, distribution, know-how, data rights where evidenced | Commodity dependency or easy imitation; investigate a path, not a mature moat requirement |
| Market and expansion path | Can the initial use case support a larger opportunity? | Specific initial buyer and plausible adjacent use cases | Large market headline without a reachable entry point |
| Why now | What recent change matters to this specific company? | Dated enabling capability, customer behavior, product milestone, or other evidenced catalyst | Trend language without causal link; a recent post alone is not a catalyst |
| Evidence sufficiency | Are the decisive claims supported enough for the next action? | Relevant, attributable, sufficiently current observations | Missing, stale, dependent, or conflicting evidence; request a specific check |
| Actionability | Can a reviewer identify whom to approach and why? | Corroborated founder role, public route, factual rationale | Name collision, uncertain role, or missing contact route; hold outreach readiness |

Use dimension labels **supported strength / mixed / supported concern / unknown / not applicable**. Confidence is separately **high / moderate / low / unknown**, attached to individual claims. High confidence that a source made a statement is different from high confidence that the underlying claim is true. None of these labels predicts returns.

Assess leverage through a measurable improvement relative to an explicit baseline. A company need not have a mature moat to warrant early review. Founder velocity includes customer learning and relevant technical milestones; raw commit frequency is insufficient.

## 7. Hard constraints, preferences, and unknowns

**Human-selected review gate:** Product evidence is necessary before partner review in this demonstration. A promising idea-only founder may be discovered and investigated but cannot bypass that gate. A working private artifact can be referenced through authorized human review; lack of a public repository does not prove no product exists. Record an attestation as an attestation rather than as independently inspected proof.

**Proposed workflow constraints:** No automated outbound delivery. No invented affiliation. No unsupported founder identity in an outreach-ready package. No known YC portfolio company counted as a new opportunity for YC. These govern workflow integrity rather than an investment conclusion.

**Soft preferences:** Specific founder accomplishments, demonstrated learning, distinctive insight, technical leverage, clear user value, and a credible initial wedge. Technical search lanes have priority but do not disqualify other sectors.

**Not selected or not established:** Geographic exclusions, fixed founder count, elite education/employer requirements, revenue floors, maximum capital intensity, mandatory AI exposure, numeric fit cutoffs, or an automatic Series B rejection. Stage mismatch should be flagged against the chosen sourcing focus; a deterministic stage exclusion requires explicit approval of that policy and reliable stage evidence.

For each constraint, record **satisfied / contradicted / unknown / not applicable**. Unknown never means satisfied or failed. Distinguish a true incompatibility from an unmet evidence requirement: failure to find a product artifact can hold review without proving that no product exists.

## 8. Evidence protocol

Each material observation records: company/entity identifiers; claim; exact URL or provider record reference; source author and type; publication/event date if known; access time; evidence mode; supported and challenged criteria; confidence rationale; limitations; and superseded observations.

Evidence modes include **directly inspected artifact**, **first-party claim**, **independent observation**, **provider-reported field**, **human attestation**, **inference**, and **illustrative fixture**. A company announcement proves what was announced, not independently that it worked. Two pages copying one press release are one underlying claim.

| Claim | Appropriate check | Insufficient by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Founder identity and current role | Consistent company-linked public profile or explicit company attribution; resolve material conflicts | Name-only match or repository handle |
| Product exists and founder contribution | Working demonstration, prototype, technical artifact, or equivalent inspected evidence with ownership attribution | Landing page, pitch deck, forks, stars, or waitlist alone |
| Shipping/learning velocity | Dated meaningful changes and explanation of what was learned | Commit count, contribution heatmap, or promotional posts |
| User demand or traction | Attributed feedback or metrics with definitions, dates, and source limitations | Unverified logos, signups conflated with active use, cumulative totals without a period |
| Technical advantage | Relevant baseline, evaluation conditions, limitations, and comparable outcomes | Unreproducible superiority claim |
| Funding stage and YC status | Dated first-party financing/program evidence and checked entity identity; reconcile provider conflicts | Missing directory entry treated as proof of no funding or no YC backing |
| Why-now catalyst | Dated event plus an explicit causal link to this opportunity | Broad market hype or a fresh article about an old event |

Conflicts trigger reconciliation of entity, time, definition, and original source. A private metric remains unknown when unavailable. No universal freshness window is imposed: recheck volatile fields before use and retain historical observations as historical.

Every material gap generates an investigation request: **question; reason it matters; current evidence; best accessible source; owner; limit/deadline; and what each plausible answer would change**. Spend effort where it can alter a disposition. Close a loop when a material answer arrives or the declared limit is reached.

## 9. Case lifecycle and decision rules

Keep **workflow state**, **fit judgment**, and **run completion reason** distinct. This is a conceptual contract for discussion, not a required graph or database design.

| State | Entry and exit meaning |
|---|---|
| Discovered | Normalize identity; log discovery source/date; deduplicate against the declared known universe. |
| Investigating | Assess fit and pursue the most consequential unresolved questions. |
| Needs Evidence | Name the material gap and a bounded request. Return to investigation when evidence arrives. |
| Partner Review Ready | Product gate met, identity sufficiently resolved for review, and an evidence-backed fit case can be articulated. Missing nonessential traction can remain explicit. |
| Qualified | Human partner/reviewer chooses to progress the case. This is neither investment approval nor YC acceptance. |
| Outreach Ready | Qualified case has corroborated current founder identity, appropriate public contact route, supported why-company/why-now rationale, caveats, and a factual draft ready for human review. |
| Monitor | A specific observable event could change the next action; record trigger, owner, and revisit condition. |
| Rejected | An approved incompatibility or sufficiently supported pass rationale is recorded. Reopen only for material new evidence or a mandate change. |

**Evidence exhausted** and **budget exhausted** describe why work stopped; they are not negative fit judgments. Preserve the last state, unresolved claims, attempts, and restart condition. Do not count stalled cases toward an outreach-ready target.

An immutable assessment history is a proposed product requirement; this document makes no claim that it is implemented. Later information creates a new assessment with a change reason rather than rewriting what was known before.

## 10. Candidate case output

Every reviewable case should contain:

1. Company and product identity; founder names/roles and corroboration status; as-of time.
2. Plain-language product, target user, and current alternative.
3. Mandate version, discovery route, deduplication result, YC-backing status, and novelty limits.
4. Independent dimension assessments with supporting and contrary observations.
5. Constraint statuses and product-evidence gate result.
6. Why this company, why now, why these founders — separating fact from interpretation.
7. Strongest reason to pass, decisive unknown, and next investigation.
8. Proposed state or human decision, decision owner, and rationale.
9. For outreach readiness: public contact route, message draft, and unresolved caveats visible to the reviewer.

Suggested message structure: identify the sender accurately; reference an evidenced artifact or milestone; explain a specific reason for interest; ask a modest, relevant question. Do not imply an invitation, acceptance, inside knowledge, or permission to speak for YC.

## 11. Illustrative dispositions

All cases below are invented teaching scenarios. They are not claims about actual founders, sourced companies, or implemented behavior.

| Scenario | Assessment and next action |
|---|---|
| A solo builder has a working developer tool and dated releases responding to user issues; revenue is unavailable | Potential partner-review case once attribution and YC-status checks are adequate. Revenue stays unknown. Solo status does not cause a pass. |
| An articulate domain expert has a compelling problem analysis and waitlist, but no evidenced prototype | Needs Evidence under the human-selected product gate. Investigate a demonstration; do not reject simply because traction is missing. |
| An AI repository has attention, but its account owner cannot be tied to the claimed founder and the benchmark is unclear | Investigate identity and baseline. Stars do not clear product attribution or actionability. |
| A hardware team has an attributable working prototype, no software repository, and a long development cycle | Investigate milestone credibility and user need. Apply equivalent product evidence; do not impose software release cadence. |
| A company is confirmed already YC-backed | Mark known YC portfolio/reference, deduplicate, and do not count as a novel YC sourcing result. This is a novelty outcome rather than a judgment of company quality. |
| Two first-party records materially disagree on the current founder role | Hold outreach readiness, reconcile dates and identity, and preserve both observations. |
| An appropriately evidenced case is reviewed and the human passes because the proposed customer wedge is unconvincing | Rejected with the review rationale and evidence. The record should say a demo reviewer passed, not that YC rejected it. |
| Sources run out while product ownership remains unresolved | Evidence exhausted; preserve uncertainty and restart condition rather than converting the gap to a negative fit score. |

## 12. Demonstration and operating review

Recommended three-minute story: show the mandate; show a product-evidenced case ready for review; inspect a promising case held for a precise missing artifact; show a clearly labeled evidence update and why it changes the next action; end with a human-reviewable outreach package and an honest target count. Synthetic illustrations and captured observations must remain visibly labeled.

Assess the service on traceable claims, useful investigations, resolved identities, novelty within the declared known universe, and defensible dispositions. Do not optimize merely for lead volume or infer investment accuracy from a demonstration.

**Next:** Select a small set of genuinely sourced cases with integration owners once actual provider observations are available. Validate the product gate and one evidence-driven state change. This research chat makes no paid calls or implementation changes.

**Later:** Add investment-time historical references, broader discovery coverage, and learning from human review. Revising search priorities must not silently rewrite approved constraints. A real fund deployment would require its own mandate and authorization.

Remaining review choices: whether seed is a strict upper stage boundary or a default search focus; who may mark Qualified; what counts toward the run target; per-run limits and monitoring ownership. Current recommendation: count only outreach-ready cases, keep qualification human, and leave numerical limits unset until chosen.
