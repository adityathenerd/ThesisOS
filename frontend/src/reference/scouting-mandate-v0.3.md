# Biosciences and Healthtech: Core Scouting Thesis

Version 0.3 | 24 October 2026, IST | Draft for human review

**Use:** Investment judgment and comparison guide for a hypothetical YC scouting workflow. Independently drafted, YC-informed, and not YC-endorsed. This document replaces the broad reference draft as the proposed primary agent input. It does not activate policy or prescribe implementation.

**Authority:** The human selected a hypothetical workflow for YC and product evidence before partner review. Following feedback that the broad draft could not anchor real comparison, the human selected equal attention to healthtech and biosciences, including therapeutics, with separate evaluation criteria. The sector interpretation, investment beliefs, search lanes, and decision rules below are proposed operator policy, not claims about YC's own sector mandate.

## 1. The investment thesis

**Seek early-stage teams turning a costly bottleneck in care delivery, biological research, or disease treatment into a repeatable product, research platform, or therapeutic asset. Prioritize specific founder insight, attributable technical or experimental execution, and evidence that the approach improves on a relevant alternative. The initial opportunity must have a credible route to adoption or development, and the next milestone must resolve a material uncertainty.**

We are willing to investigate incomplete businesses and incomplete science. We are less interested in generic healthcare branding, technology without a defined use, impressive credentials without attributable work, or platform ambition without a first application.

The central company-level question is: **What can this team do for a defined user or patient population that the current approach cannot do well, what evidence supports that claim, and what must become true next?**

### Proposed investment beliefs

| Belief | What the agent should seek | What would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| A precise bottleneck is a better entry point than a broad platform claim | A named workflow, experiment, indication, or decision; a specific current alternative | A large market description without a first use case |
| Domain insight matters when translated into a product or scientific result | Founders can explain why the bottleneck persists and demonstrate their approach | Credentials and advisor logos substitute for execution |
| Repeatability creates a route beyond bespoke work | Reusable software, instrument, assay, dataset capability, or developable asset | Each engagement depends on new founder labor without a credible repeatable core |
| Technical leverage must produce an operational or biological advantage | Measured benefit against an appropriate baseline, with limitations | Model novelty, accuracy headlines, or AI labels disconnected from useful outcomes |
| Early capital should buy a decisive learning milestone | A next experiment, deployment, validation, or development step whose result changes the case | More data, hiring, or publicity without a decision the work will resolve |

These are the convictions of this proposed mandate. They are hypotheses for the human to adopt or revise, not empirical claims that these characteristics guarantee returns.

## 2. Boundaries and opportunity allocation

Healthtech and biosciences are **equally core**. Do not automatically rank software over wet-lab companies because revenue or public demonstrations are easier to observe. Cover both in discovery; surface a coverage shortfall if one produces fewer researchable leads. No numeric allocation or score weight is selected.

Discover idea-stage, pre-seed, and seed opportunities globally. Funding labels, amounts, headcount, and incorporation age are context rather than universal quality thresholds. Later-stage financing prompts a scope/exception review rather than a newly invented automatic rejection. An idea-only company can enter investigation but cannot meet the selected review gate.

For this sector-focused draft, confirmed unrelated companies are outside the mandate. Generic consultancies, conventional staffing, ordinary clinics, commodity distribution, and wellness marketing are outside the proposed target **unless a repeatable differentiated product or asset is demonstrated**. A services business with an emerging reusable core requires investigation, not keyword rejection. Nonhealth agricultural or industrial biology is adjacent and needs a scope exception; human-health-enabling research tools are core.

Known YC-backed companies are historical/reference or known-company cases, not novel YC prospects. Unverified backing is unknown. Novelty means new to the declared known-company universe, never proof of being unknown to YC.

## 3. Four comparison tracks

Assign a primary track by the first product or asset and its value-capture model. Add a secondary track if necessary. A drug-discovery company selling software is not interchangeable with one developing its own medicines.

| Track | Entry opportunities | User, buyer, and alternative | Reason to pursue | Reason to deprioritize |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H — Healthcare delivery and operations | Referral management; authorizations; clinical documentation; tissue/procurement workflows; scheduling; care coordination; health-data infrastructure | Name actual staff/user, budget owner, and current workflow | Specific operational pain; deployable product; plausible customer access; measurable workflow benefit | Generic assistant; unclear budget owner; integration or review burden erases benefit |
| R — Bioscience research and enabling tools | Bioinformatics; microscopy analysis; lab automation; experimental design; assays/instruments; bioprocessing and manufacturing tools | Scientist/operator as user; lab/biotech/pharma as buyer; existing tool or manual process as alternative | Repeatable improvement in research, reproducibility, throughput, or cost | Consultancy presented as a platform; inaccessible inputs; irrelevant benchmark; no reusable core |
| D — Diagnostics and clinical devices | Tests, diagnostic software, monitoring, therapeutic devices | Specify intended use, patient population, clinical user, buyer/payer, and comparator | Credible prototype plus evidence relevant to its intended setting and adoption path | Performance claim disconnected from intended population; unclear clinical action or route to use |
| T — Therapeutics and drug-development platforms | Defined therapeutic assets; target/modality platforms with an initial disease program; differentiated discovery approaches linked to assets or partnerships | Patient population and treatment alternative; developer, licensor, pharma partner, and eventual access model as relevant | Plausible disease-specific rationale; attributable experiments; credible ownership/access and next development step | Platform slogan without program; prediction without experimental support; model result portrayed as patient benefit |

Tracks are equally legitimate; they require different proof. Do not compare a preclinical program's revenue with a software company's subscriptions as though the two express the same maturity.

## 4. Founder thesis: judge contribution and learning

Prefer founders who combine direct understanding of the relevant problem with control over the core learning loop. That can be a clinician and engineer, an experienced operator and builder, experimental and computational scientists, a device engineer with clinical access, or a solo founder with evidenced execution. These are examples, not team-composition requirements.

| Founder criterion | Stronger evidence | Weaker evidence | Targeted question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem proximity | Attributable work in the workflow, patient setting, or research problem; concrete observations that shaped the approach | Prestigious employer or degree with no link to the problem | What did this founder observe that changed the product or scientific hypothesis? |
| Contribution | Product/experiment authorship and specific design decisions; documented role in the work | Company title, coauthorship, or advisor relationship alone | Which core work did they own, and what can the team reproduce? |
| Execution and learning | A sequence of meaningful releases, experiments, or deployments and what changed after each | Commit frequency, paper count, or press frequency | What did the last result disprove, and what did the team change? |
| Access and coordination | Credible access to users, samples, facilities, collaborators, or development expertise | Unnamed partners or unconfirmed future access | What access exists now, under whose control, and what is still contingent? |
| Candor and judgment | Acknowledges failure modes and names a decisive test | Unqualified certainty or unsupported superiority | What is the strongest alternative explanation for the result? |

Outsourced manufacturing, CRO studies, clinical partnerships, and university facilities do not by themselves weaken founder fit. Investigate whether the company controls experimental design, interprets results, retains necessary rights, and can repeat the work. Do not require every capability to be in-house.

## 5. Shared comparison criteria

For every criterion report **supported / mixed / contradicted / unknown / not applicable**, evidence IDs, interpretation, and the fact that could change the judgment. Evidence confidence is a separate field.

| ID | Criterion | Strong fit looks like | Weak fit looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Specific problem and entry point | Named workflow or indication, current alternative, and affected user/population | Category label or market size in place of a problem |
| C2 | Founder advantage | Problem-linked, attributable capability and relevant access | Credentials without contribution or practical access |
| C3 | Distinctive mechanism | Explains how a particular approach changes an important outcome | Generic ease-of-use or AI claim |
| C4 | Product/experimental execution | Track-appropriate artifact/result that can be inspected and attributed | Only a roadmap, pitch, or unexamined claim |
| C5 | Relevant advantage | Benefit against a suitable alternative, with test context and limitations | Cherry-picked benchmark or benefit without a baseline |
| C6 | Adoption or development path | Reachable buyer/user or a credible path from current program to the next development decision | Technology with no practical way to deploy, develop, sell, or partner |
| C7 | Repeatability and future defensibility | Reusable core, know-how, workflow embedding, or asset/data rights where evidenced | Commodity resale or unbounded bespoke labor |
| C8 | Next milestone and resources | Concrete de-risking step, required access/resources, and decision consequence | Indefinite platform building with no falsifiable milestone |

Treat C1–C8 as the comparison vocabulary, not additive points. Unknown C5 is a request for relevant validation; it is not zero advantage. A supported contradiction must name the contrary fact rather than merely a failed search.

**Why now** is an additional explanation: identify a dated scientific, product, customer, or infrastructure development and explain why it changes this opportunity. A funding announcement can justify rechecking a company; it does not prove customer demand or scientific validity. Do not invent a catalyst when none is evidenced.

## 6. Track-specific evidence requirements

### H — Healthcare delivery and operations

Establish the exact workflow, who performs it, who pays, and what happens before and after the product. Inspect an actual workflow example or attributable deployment evidence. Investigate operational benefit, error/review burden, integration work, and whether the buying process is reachable. Where patient-affecting decisions are involved, route clinical claims to the appropriate specialist rather than treating productivity as clinical validation.

Compare using task completion, user behavior, time/cost, failure handling, or deployment evidence relevant to the claim. A letter of intent is interest rather than active use. A pilot is not automatically a paid contract. No revenue floor is required.

### R — Bioscience research and enabling tools

Establish the research operation and its baseline. Inspect worked inputs/outputs, an attributable prototype, assay data, or a relevant experimental demonstration. Investigate repeatability, dataset/sample suitability, user workflow, cost and throughput, installation/access requirements, and the difference between custom services and a reusable tool.

Computational novelty needs evidence linked to the research task. Experimental tools need evidence appropriate to the claimed capability. Where commercialization depends on university IP, external datasets, samples, or instrumentation, record control/access as supported, unknown, or contradicted. Do not infer commercial rights from publication.

### D — Diagnostics and clinical devices

Record intended use, population, setting, reference/comparator, and proposed action following the result. Inspect the prototype and the most relevant validation available, preserving study design, sample context, endpoints, and limitations. Separate analytical performance, clinical performance, and practical utility. A product demo cannot establish all three.

Regulatory status and route to use must be tied to the actual jurisdiction and product claim, verified from authoritative records or left for specialist review. Do not require a clearance number merely because a company mentions healthcare, and do not infer clearance from registration or a clinical partnership. These are investigation rules, not a regulatory determination.

For US devices, FDA explicitly distinguishes registration/listing from approval, clearance, or authorization. [FDA: Registration and Listing Reminders](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-registration-and-listing/important-reminders-about-registration-and-listing), accessed 24 October 2026.

### T — Therapeutics and drug-development platforms

Record the initial indication, target/biological rationale, modality or platform role, lead program maturity, and relevant standard of care or development alternative. Inspect actual experimental findings, with their model, controls/comparator, endpoint, and limitations. A computational prediction or discovery press release alone does not satisfy the proposed experimental-evidence gate.

For an early discovery program, relevant measured mechanistic evidence may justify specialist partner review without a selected clinical candidate. For a nominated asset, investigate potency/selectivity, exposure, safety, manufacturing, or other claim-relevant development risks as appropriate to that asset. Do not impose late-stage evidence on early programs or mark unperformed studies as failures.

Investigate asset ownership/license status, access to core samples/models, development leadership, and the next decisive experiment or development milestone. Capture what could falsify the program and what resources the next test requires. Human specialist review determines whether early evidence is persuasive; the scouting agent does not certify efficacy.

Preclinical findings and human clinical evidence are different categories. The FDA's explanatory material distinguishes the purposes of preclinical research and studies in people; it does not justify assuming that a company with an experimental result has established clinical benefit. [FDA: Preclinical Research](https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-2-preclinical-research), [FDA: Clinical Research](https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-3-clinical-research). These sources describe US drug development; they are not a universal pathway for all products or jurisdictions.

## 7. What counts as product evidence here

The human's product-evidence requirement is retained. Its proposed translation into this sector mandate is:

| Track | Evidence that can meet the gate after inspection and attribution | Does not meet it alone |
|---|---|---|
| H | Working workflow demonstration or attributable use/deployment record showing the claimed functionality | Landing page, waiting list, buyer logo, future pilot announcement |
| R | Working tool with a relevant worked example; functioning prototype; or measured experimental capability | Tool description, paper title, patents, promised data access |
| D | Functioning prototype/test plus an attributable result relevant to its intended capability | Concept render or uncontextualized accuracy number |
| T | Inspectable experimental findings tied to an initial biological program or platform capability | Docking/model predictions, a platform diagram, or financing alone |

An inspectable scientific report is evidence of what it reports, with the report's limitations. This does not imply an independently repeated experiment. Gate results are **met / partly evidenced / unknown / contradicted**, with a mode describing what was actually inspected. Human attestations must remain labeled; private material cannot be treated as inspected unless authorized access occurred.

The therapeutics/diagnostics interpretation of this gate is proposed for human review. It replaces an inappropriate software-MVP expectation while retaining the requirement for demonstrated work.

## 8. Discovery instructions an agent can act on

Search by **problem + buyer/user + artifact or milestone**, then expand using adjacent terminology. Sector tags and keyword matches generate leads; they do not establish thesis fit.

| Track | Query families / synonyms | High-value source routes |
|---|---|---|
| H | referral intake; prior authorization; tissue ordering; revenue-cycle workflow; clinical documentation; care coordination + provider, hospital, clinic, payer + demo, deployment, pilot, seed | Company product/docs; attributable deployment cases; founder/operator interviews; investor announcements |
| R | microscopy analysis; microbial genomics; bioinformatics; assay automation; lab robotics; bioprocess analytics + scientist, biotech, pharma, laboratory + benchmark, tutorial, prototype, spinout, seed | Product tutorials/docs; scientific methods/results; university commercialization pages; company and investor records |
| D | diagnostic assay; point-of-care; clinical monitoring; medical device + intended use/indication + prototype, validation, clinical study | Company technical reports; published studies; appropriate official study/product records |
| T | indication or disease + target/modality or platform + functional assay, preclinical, perturbation, translational, spinout, seed | Original studies/preprints; company science and pipeline; university licensing/spinout records; sponsor and investor announcements |

For founder-first scouting, identify contributors to relevant working products or experiments, establish whether they are building a company, and verify their actual role. Do not label every paper author a founder or every scientific advisor an operator. GitHub is one route, not the default evidence universe for biology.

For every promising lead retrieve the current company product/science page, team/identity source, and dated milestone or financing source. Check for newer developments before deciding stage. Record inaccessible sources and unverified fields instead of claiming absence.

## 9. Decision and comparison rules

1. Resolve company identity and assign primary/secondary track. If scope is ambiguous, investigate the first product and customer/asset model.
2. Check known YC backing and the declared known-company list. Confirmed known entities exit the novel-prospect pool without receiving a negative investment judgment.
3. Build C1–C8 with evidence and track-specific requirements. Separate source claims, inspected artifacts, and independent observations.
4. **Priority investigate:** specific in-scope use/program, attributable relevant team, and a meaningful product/experimental signal or a well-defined gap that can plausibly be resolved. Name why this lead deserves the next research action.
5. **Needs evidence:** a plausible thesis match has a material unresolved requirement. State the exact missing fact and what each answer would change.
6. **Partner review ready:** the product gate is met; the core problem/program, founder contribution, and differentiated approach are supported enough to articulate; material contradictions are resolved or explicitly accepted by the human reviewer; and remaining scientific/commercial risks are visible. Use specialist partner review for D/T claims.
7. **Monitor:** identify a concrete future catalyst such as an attributable prototype, experimental result, licensing decision, or relevant deployment. Record the trigger and owner.
8. **Pass/outside mandate:** cite confirmed sector mismatch, lack of a repeatable product/asset after investigation, or a supported human pass rationale. Stage exceptions require review. Failed searches, unavailable clinical data at an early stage, and unknown revenue are not rejection reasons.

For pairwise comparison, compare companies within the same primary track and comparable milestone first. Explain which has the better-supported problem/approach, team contribution, relevant advantage, adoption/development path, and next milestone. Identify any comparison that cannot be made. Across tracks, present a balanced slate and ask for portfolio allocation judgment; do not manufacture one total rank that favors easier-to-observe software.

Fit and readiness are separate: a company can be a strong thesis match with insufficient evidence, or well documented with weak fit. More evidence alone does not make a company more attractive. Evidence/budget exhaustion are work-stop reasons, not pass judgments.

## 10. Required comparison output

For each company return: identity and as-of date; current role-verified founders/operators; primary/secondary track and rationale; initial problem/program and alternative; C1–C8 findings with evidence; product-gate result and evidence mode; stage and YC-status checks; best reason to pursue; strongest reason to pass; decisive unknown; next investigation and decision consequence; recommendation and reviewer; and why-now status.

The investment rationale must complete this sentence with specifics:

> We would investigate [company] because [defined bottleneck] is addressed by [specific approach], and [attributed evidence] supports [limited conclusion]. The decisive uncertainty is [gap]. The next useful check is [action], which would change [decision].

## 11. YC grounding and human review

The inherited founder/insight orientation is informed by [Paul Graham's application guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply). [YC's RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs) provides thematic prompts, not an exhaustive selection policy. The health/bio specialization and detailed criteria in this document are our proposed mandate choices; portfolio resemblance is not a trained investment rule.

Review these decisions first: Does the central investment bet reflect your intent? Does track-appropriate experimental evidence satisfy your product gate for therapeutics and diagnostics? Should the proposed boundary around conventional services and nonhealth biology stand? Exact run targets, budgets, and check sizes remain unset. No geographic, pedigree, revenue, or fixed founder-count restriction is introduced.
